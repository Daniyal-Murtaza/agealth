For the parent: a phone that rings

At the time she chose, in her language, in the same warm voice. Sleep, meals, medication, then whatever she wants to talk about. It remembers yesterday: "did the plumber come?" She can call it too, any time, from a number saved in her phone as "Sunday".

For the family: a quiet page

A short note after each call, alerts when it matters, and a place to leave what you'd like asked tomorrow. "Remind Dad about the cardiologist." "Ask if she wants us for Thanksgiving." Siblings stop coordinating by group text.

Safe by code, not by prompt

"I fell", "chest pain", "can't breathe" are caught before any AI runs. The call stays on the line, keeps her calm, and every family member is alerted before the next sentence is spoken. Nothing is ever invented: every note quotes her own words.

What a call sounds like

Short turns. One question at a time. Her name now and then, not every sentence.

Sunday follows a loose plan (how are you, sleep, medication, meals, anything the family left, then open talk) and never asks twice for something she already said. Highlighted words are what the note to the family was built from.

  • SundayHello Mom, it's Sunday calling, the daily call from your son Daniel. How are you today?
  • MomI'm alright, honey. Slept a bit late but took my pill with my coffee.
  • SundayI'm glad you took your pill. Sarah asked me to tell you she'll call on Thursday after work. And Daniel wants to know: do you want a ride to the clinic on Tuesday?
  • MomYes, I'll take the ride, after eleven though. Linda brought banana bread yesterday.
  • SundayAfter eleven on Tuesday, I'll tell him. Did you enjoy the banana bread?
  • MomVery much. The plumber still hasn't come, third day now.
  • SundayThat's frustrating. I'll ask you tomorrow whether he showed up. How are Emma and Noah?

How it works

  1. You introduce SundayAdd your parent, her language and call time, and who in the family gets which alerts. Have Sunday place the first call while you're with her.
  2. The phone rings"Hello Mom, it's Sunday, the daily call from your son Daniel. How are you today?" Five to twelve minutes, one question at a time.
  3. You get the noteMood, medication, anything mentioned, anything worth a look. Replies to what you asked. By text or WhatsApp, with a link to read the call.
  4. Patterns over weeksEvery Sunday evening: how the week went, what to follow up on, what to talk about on your own call. Plain language, never a diagnosis.

Three rules, enforced in code

Mom never touches technology. No app, no account, nothing to charge. Any phone that rings, including the landline.
Safety does not depend on the AI. Keyword triggers run first; emergencies follow a script and alert the family at once.
Nothing is invented. Every observation, alert and memory must quote the parent, or it is dropped.

Trust, visible

Always says it is an assistant sent by the family. Never gives medical advice. Never asks for money, card numbers or codes, and tells the parent so, which also trains them to hang up on real scammers. Recording only with the parent's consent, announced on every recorded call. Questions families ask

Will my mother know it's not a person?

Yes, always. The first words of every call are "it's Sunday, the daily call from your son". If she asks, it says it is an assistant. It never pretends otherwise.

What if she doesn't answer?

Sunday tries once more fifteen minutes later, then leaves her alone for the day. Two mornings in a row with no answer and you get a "worth a call" note; three and the whole family gets an urgent alert.

What happens if she says she has fallen?

Before the AI is even consulted, every family member gets an urgent text quoting her words. Sunday stays on the line, tells her help is being called, and reminds her she can dial 911. It does not hang up or call 911 itself.

Does it give medical advice?

Never. It shows concern, asks how she is managing, and tells the family. The doctor is the right person to ask, and Sunday says so.

Who can see the notes?

Only the family members you add, each at the level they choose: everything, check-today and urgent, or urgent only. Sign-in is a one-time link to their own phone. You can export or delete everything at any time. Privacy.

Which languages, and where?

English and Spanish, plus Urdu, Hindi and Punjabi, including the emergency phrases in each. The family's notes are in English. Sunday calls numbers in the United States; we'll say so plainly if a number is outside that.

What does it cost?

Free while we are in beta. Plans are published so you know what comes next.
